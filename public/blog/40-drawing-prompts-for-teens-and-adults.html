40 Drawing Prompts for Teens and Adults

When you're past "draw a cat" but still staring at a blank page — here are 40 prompts with a little more depth, organized by category.

Home › Drawing Ideas › 40 Prompts for Teens & Adults

The hardest part of drawing is rarely the drawing itself — it's deciding what to draw. Blank-page paralysis is real, and it hits experienced artists just as hard as beginners. The prompts below are designed to give you a starting point with enough of a twist that you have to actually think and interpret, rather than just copy something literally.

Pick one, set a timer for 15–20 minutes, and see where it goes. You don't need to finish it — a strong sketch is worth more than a rushed "complete" drawing.

Surreal & Conceptual

A staircase that leads into the sky
A city built inside a jar
A clock made of plants
A door floating in the middle of the ocean
A shadow that doesn't match its owner
A tree with houses instead of leaves
A person made entirely of smoke
A room where gravity works sideways

Everyday Objects, Reimagined

A backpack that's actually alive
An umbrella in a world with no rain
A phone from 200 years in the future
A chair with a personality
A key that opens something impossible
A lamp that lights up emotions instead of rooms

Characters & Portraits

A villain who thinks they're the hero
A character defined by one object they carry
Someone mid-laugh, not posed
A knight who's afraid of swords
A robot with an old soul
Two characters who are opposites but best friends
A self-portrait using only shapes, no lines

A mountain from a bird's-eye view
A single leaf, drawn extremely large
A desert with one unexpected object in it
An underwater cave with bioluminescent light
A tree that's survived a storm

Something warm and cozy on a cold day
A place that feels abandoned but safe

A quiet 3am kitchen

Design & Function

A logo for a business that doesn't exist yet
A vehicle designed for a world with no roads
A board game box cover for an imaginary game
An album cover for a genre you invent
A poster for a movie that hasn't been made
A piece of furniture that solves a problem you actually have

A flag for a country you make up
💡 Pro Tip: If a prompt makes you pause and think "wait, how would that even work?" — that's usually a sign it's a good one. The prompts that are instantly obvious tend to produce forgettable drawings. The ones that require a little problem-solving almost always produce more interesting results.

Stuck Between Two Ideas?

A useful trick: pick two prompts from different categories and combine them. "A staircase that leads into the sky" plus "someone mid-laugh, not posed" might turn into a character laughing at the top of an impossible staircase. Combining unrelated prompts is one of the fastest ways to land on something you wouldn't have thought of drawing on your own.
