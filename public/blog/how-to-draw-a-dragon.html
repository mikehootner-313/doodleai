How to Draw a Dragon Step by Step

A friendly, cartoon-style dragon that's much easier to draw than it looks — great for kids who love fantasy creatures.

Home › Drawing Ideas › How to Draw a Dragon

Dragons feel like they should be complicated — scales, wings, claws, all of it. But a friendly cartoon dragon is really just a lizard shape with a few extra features layered on top. Once you break it into stages, it's one of the more forgiving fantasy creatures to draw, because dragons don't have to look "correct" the way a real animal does. There's no wrong way to draw a creature that doesn't exist.

What You'll Need

  • A pencil
  • Paper or a sketchbook
  • An eraser
  • Colored pencils or markers for scales and details

Step-by-Step Instructions

Step 1

Draw the Head

Start with an oval for the head, a little longer than it is wide, angled slightly upward like the dragon is looking up. This gives the whole pose energy right from the first shape.

Step 2

Add the Snout

Extend a rounded, slightly curved shape forward from the lower part of the head for the snout. Keep it soft and rounded for a friendly look, or make it longer and more angular for a fiercer one.

Step 3

Sketch the Body

Draw a long, curved oval trailing back from the head for the body — think of it like an "S" curve rather than a straight line, which gives the dragon a sense of movement. The body should taper as it goes, wider near the head and narrower toward the tail.

Step 4

Add the Wings

On the dragon's back, sketch two large wing shapes — a curved main strut with a scalloped, batlike edge trailing behind it. Wings are one of the most fun parts to draw, so feel free to make them big and dramatic.

Step 5

Draw the Legs and Claws

Add four short, sturdy legs coming off the body, each ending in a small paw with three or four curved claw lines. Bent legs give the dragon a crouched, ready-to-pounce feel; straight legs give it a calmer standing pose.

Step 6

Finish the Tail

Continue the body line out into a long, tapering tail. Add a simple triangle or arrow shape at the very tip for a classic dragon-tail spike.

Step 7

Add Spikes and Face Details

Draw a row of small triangle spikes running from the head down the spine to the tail. Add two round eyes with small pupils, nostril dots on the snout, and — if you want a happy dragon — a curved smiling mouth line.

Step 8

Clean Up and Add Texture

Erase your guide lines, then go over your final outline a bit darker. Add small curved lines across the body to suggest scales — you don't need to cover the whole dragon, just enough in a few areas to imply texture.

Coloring Your Dragon

  • Green with a lighter green belly for a classic forest dragon
  • Red and orange for a fire-breathing look
  • Blue and purple for an ice or night dragon
  • Gold and bronze for a treasure-guarding dragon
💡 Pro Tip: Want your dragon to look like it's breathing fire? Add a swirl of jagged, overlapping triangle and curve shapes in orange, yellow, and red coming out of the mouth. It's easier than it looks and instantly makes the drawing feel alive.

Give Your Dragon a Personality

Small changes make a big difference in how a dragon "feels." Rounder shapes, big eyes, and a smiling mouth make a friendly, kid-movie dragon. Sharper angles, narrow eyes, and visible teeth make a fiercer, more dramatic one. Try drawing the same dragon shape twice with different details and see how differently the two versions read.
